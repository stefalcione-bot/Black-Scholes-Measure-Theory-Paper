## 📄 Overview

This paper bridges the gap between measure-theoretic probability theory and continuous-time financial mathematics. Starting from the construction of abstract measurable spaces and Lebesgue integration, the work builds up through filtrations, martingales, Itô calculus, and stochastic differential equations (SDEs), culminating in the derivation of the Black-Scholes European call pricing formula and sensitivity analysis (Greeks) under an equivalent martingale measure.

## 📌 Abstract

This paper introduces foundational elements of multivariate measure theory and probability, aimed at providing a rigorous path toward stochastic models used in finance. Beginning from the construction of probability spaces and integration with respect to measures, the work presents the definition of random variables, expectations, and filtrations. Building on these, it develops Brownian motion and the concept of geometric Brownian motion as a model for asset prices. 

The paper then introduces the concept of martingales and explores change of measure techniques (Girsanov's theorem) which are essential in stochastic modeling and risk-neutral pricing. The final section applies these ideas to the Black-Scholes model, showing how the risk-neutral measure leads to a consistent and arbitrage-free valuation framework.
